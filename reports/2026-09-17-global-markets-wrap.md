## TL;DR

Markets spent today undoing yesterday's damage. Wednesday's FOMC meeting (first Fed hike since July 2023, hawkish 16-of-18 dot plot) had sent the Dow down 1.2%, the 10-year Treasury yield briefly above 5%, and oil spiking on Middle East supply fears. Thursday reversed a lot of that:

1. **A relief rally, not a new bullish catalyst.** Oil eased further (Brent -1.8% to -2.9% depending on snapshot), and Treasury yields fell across the curve (10-year down ~7bp to 4.93–4.95%, back under the 5% "danger zone" investors had been watching) as bond buyers concluded the post-Fed selloff had reached "maximum pain." A stronger-than-expected weekly jobless-claims print (196K vs. 207K expected) reinforced the view that the labor market isn't cracking even as the Fed tightens. The S&P 500 rose **+1.14%** — its best day in six weeks — the Nasdaq **+1.69%**, and the VIX fell **-12.8%** to 15.44. Chip and memory stocks led hard: Intel (+7.7% to +10% depending on the print), AMD (+6–7%), Micron and SanDisk (+5–7%), on a mix of AI-optimism bounce-back from Monday's AI-slowdown selloff and Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang saying next year's chip sales will double this year's.
2. **Turkey's stock market stabilized after a coordinated government rescue.** Following Tuesday–Wednesday's Pusula Portföy/Tera Portföy fund-redemption crisis (a ~10% three-day BIST 100 drop, circuit breakers, a run on Turkish investment funds), Turkey's Financial Stability Committee, central bank, and capital-markets regulator (SPK) intervened Thursday: the central bank boosted repo funding to 300B lira and raised interbank borrowing limits tenfold, the SPK ordered liquidation of ~$21.4B in troubled funds across seven portfolio managers, margin requirements were eased, and the sovereign wealth fund bought blue-chip equities directly. The Turkey ETF (TUR) rose **+4.20%** on the day — still down 6.78% over five days — while lira and CDS moves stayed contained, consistent with this being a fund-liquidity event rather than a sovereign-credit one.
3. **Zcash (ZEC) kept running** — up another **+10.5%** today after yesterday's +18.2% governance-vote/ETF/short-squeeze rally, the second straight outsized day for the name.

**Note on context:** none of this reverses the macro backdrop — the Fed's dot plot still shows 16 of 18 officials expecting at least one more 2026 hike, and Chair Kevin Warsh's remarks Wednesday were explicitly hawkish. Today's rally is best read as a snapback from an overshoot, not a change in the rate-hike cycle everyone is still pricing.

## Thread 1: Relief rally — oil eases, Treasury yields retreat from post-Fed highs

*Source: AP (via Anchorage Daily News, The Sun Chronicle, Washington Times-Herald), Yahoo! Finance, MT Newswires, Morningstar, AOL.com, News 1 (Seoul), Financial News (Korea), all September 17, 2026.*

The S&P 500 closed **+1.14%** to 7,637.76, the Dow **+0.61%** (+316.14 pts) to 51,778.04, and the Nasdaq Composite **+1.69%** to 26,418.30 — the S&P's best single-day gain in six weeks and just its second up day in the last nine sessions. Two things drove it: Brent crude slid roughly 1–3% (settling near $103.91–$104.82 depending on the wire and snapshot time) off this week's Middle-East-driven highs near $110, and the 10-year Treasury yield fell more than 7bp to **4.93%**, down from an intraday high just above 5% recorded immediately after Wednesday's Fed decision. The 2-year yield (most sensitive to Fed policy) fell roughly 4–5bp to ~4.67–4.68%, and the 30-year fell ~6bp to ~5.28–5.30%.

Weekly initial jobless claims fell to **196,000** (from 206,000 prior week), beating expectations of an increase to 207,000 — read as continued labor-market resilience without reigniting the "hot jobs = more hikes" fear that drove the September 4 selloff, likely because the move was framed against a market already pricing further tightening. Separately, the Philadelphia Fed's manufacturing index fell to 37.8 in September from 47.4 in August, still beating the expected drop to 32.1.

AOL.com quoted market commentary framing the yield move as "yields hit maximum pain" — the Fed's unanimous 12-0 hike vote itself was read by some (Stifel's Lindsey Piegza; TruStage's Steve Rick) as credibility-positive ("the Fed regaining trust"), separate from the hawkish dot-plot content.

**Chip and memory stocks led the equity rally**, extending a recovery that began after Monday's AI-slowdown-driven selloff (tied to Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei's remarks on pacing frontier AI development): Intel rose between **+7.7%** (Twelve Data quote) and as much as +10% intraday per wire reports; AMD, Micron, and SanDisk each rose 5–7%; the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index (SOX) rose ~3%. Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang told press that AI-chip sales next year will be double this year's — cited repeatedly as a sentiment driver alongside the macro relief.

## Thread 2: Turkey's markets stabilize after coordinated state intervention

*Source: EKathimerini.com, MSN, Bne IntelliNews, Hurriyet Daily News, CNBC Arabia, Wallstreetcn, Sohu, Ifeng, all September 17, 2026.*

This week's Turkish fund-liquidity crisis (Pusula Portföy defaulting on redemptions September 15, Tera Portföy following September 16–17, a ~10% three-day BIST 100 decline and repeated circuit breakers) prompted a same-day, multi-agency response Thursday:

- **Central bank:** boosted repo funding to 300 billion lira and raised banks' interbank borrowing limits tenfold to ensure lira liquidity.
- **Capital markets regulator (SPK):** ordered the liquidation of funds managed by seven portfolio-management companies (Tera Portföy, Pusula Portföy, Hedef Portföy, and others) — a combined ~891 billion lira (~$21.4B) across ~353,000 investors — and temporarily cut the mandatory equity-maintenance ratio for margin trading from 35% to 20% through October 2.
- **Banking regulator (BDDK):** eased capital rules so banks' own share buybacks aren't deducted from core capital through year-end.
- **Sovereign wealth fund (TVF)**, via state-run Ziraat Portföy, bought BIST-30 blue-chip equities directly to absorb selling pressure.
- Separately, authorities placed a travel ban on Tera Holding's chairman and raided a brokerage tied to a holding company under investigation for alleged manipulation of specific small-cap names (Katilimevim, Gundogdu Gida, Destek Finans Faktoring).

Finance Minister Mehmet Şimşek's Financial Stability Committee characterized the episode as "a temporary and manageable credit and liquidity issue concentrated in a limited number of portfolio management companies," not a systemic one. Market reaction: the Turkey ETF (TUR) rose **+4.20%** on the day (Bigdata.com/FMP tearsheet) and the BIST 100 itself gained as much as 2.6% intraday per CNBC Arabia, with the Turkish banking sub-index up as much as 8%. TUR is still down **-6.78%** over five trading days — this is a partial stabilization, not a full recovery — and the lira and Turkish CDS moves have stayed contained throughout (consistent with a fund-industry-specific event rather than a sovereign-credit repricing), per the same read flagged in yesterday's report.

- **Whether today's relief rally is a genuine reset or a pause before the Fed's tightening bias reasserts itself.** The dot plot (16 of 18 officials expecting ≥1 more 2026 hike) hasn't changed; UBS Global Wealth Management's Mark Haefele was quoted projecting further gains "while bracing for volatility along the way."

- **Whether Turkey's fund-liquidity intervention holds.** The regulator's own framing ("temporary and manageable, concentrated in a limited number of portfolio management companies") is the optimistic case; MSCI has separately flagged concerns about shareholder transparency and coordinated trading in Turkish equities that predate this week's crisis.
- **Zcash's (ZEC) second straight >10% day.** As flagged yesterday, a short-squeeze component to this move is explicitly on the table per prior sourcing — a rally built partly on forced short-covering can reverse quickly.
- **CHPT's unexplained +11.34% move** and Bitcoin Cash's unexplained +5.52% move — both flagged above as numbers without an attributed cause; worth a scoped follow-up if either persists or matters to a position.
