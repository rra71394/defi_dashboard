# Global Markets Wrap — September 9, 2026

*Compiled from Bigdata.com (market tearsheet + news search), Twelve Data, and Alpha Vantage. Data as of ~21:35-21:40 UTC, September 9, 2026 (US cash equity close ~20:00 UTC / 4:00 PM ET).*

## TL;DR

Middle East escalation, not a new US data print, drove a third straight losing session on Wall Street. The US destroyed five Iranian oil tankers near Kharg Island on Tuesday; Iran retaliated Wednesday by striking a US base in Jordan and attacking 10 ships near the Strait of Hormuz, while Yemen's Houthis kept hitting Saudi energy infrastructure (targeting a 400kb/d refinery, per one Houthi claim) after Tuesday's strike on Saudi Aramco's Jazan facilities. Brent crossed **$100/bbl** for the first time since July (settling **$101.21, +3.4%**) and WTI settled **$96.05, +3.2%** — both roughly their highest levels in three months. That pushed the 10-year Treasury yield as high as **4.84%** intraday (highest since late 2023) and the 2-year to **4.43%** (a multi-year high), and US equities fell broadly: S&P 500 **-0.48%**, Dow **-0.77%** (~400 points, its third consecutive lower close), Nasdaq Composite **-0.62%**. The VIX jumped **+4.71%** to 16.46 — still a low absolute level, but a real move.

Two idiosyncratic stories cut against the broader tape. **Meta Platforms (+6.51%)**, a fund watchlist position, was the day's single biggest company-specific mover after launching **Muse**, its first autonomous personal AI agent, with the company saying early usage "blew way past our projections." **Marvell Technology (+4.26%)**, also a watchlist position, jumped after CEO Matt Murphy told CNBC's *Mad Money* that Marvell's two-year AI revenue outlook had risen by $6.5 billion (to a combined ~$30B across this year and next) and called the company "the Switzerland" of the AI chip market. Against that, **KLA Corp (-3.21%)** breached its alert threshold with no named catalyst found this run, and semis were otherwise mixed — Broadcom and KLA fell while AMD, Marvell, and Intel rose.

**Gold's move is worth flagging on its own**: after an intraday round-trip from a low near $4,341 to a rally near $4,435, gold settled the session **up** (+0.49% per Bigdata's futures tearsheet; some spot-price wires put it as high as +1.4%) *despite* the same rate-hike/risk-off backdrop that should pressure it, and despite oil-driven inflation fears. A softer dollar, Goldman Sachs reaffirming a **$4,900/oz** year-end target, and continued central-bank buying (the Dutch central bank disclosed moving ~86 tons of gold from New York and Ottawa to London) get the credit in today's coverage. This is a reversal of the pattern flagged in the September 4 and September 8 wraps, where gold fell alongside rising yields — worth watching whether it holds or was one day's noise ahead of Thursday's PPI and Friday's CPI, the next hard data points for the September 15-16 FOMC decision (money markets are pricing roughly a 58-60% chance of a hike).

**Meta's Muse launch, in detail** (source: MT Newswires, Benzinga, Forbes, Nasdaq/Motley Fool, all Sept 8-9): Meta launched **Muse** ("Muse," internally "Hatch"), its first autonomous personal AI agent, on Tuesday — it can send emails, book travel, fill out forms, and make purchases via Stripe's Link on a person's behalf, running on a dedicated "Muse Secure VM." He attributed the growth to "trust" built with hyperscale customers over a decade, called Marvell "the Switzerland of this entire market" (i.e., works with every major hyperscaler rather than depending on one), and referenced an Nvidia partnership (announced March) and an expanded Google agreement (August) as evidence, even after Marvell lost a separate custom-AI-chip deal to Qualcomm for Amazon's business earlier in the week. Marvell stock remains about 28% below its all-time high despite the move.

**What happened, chronologically** (source: MT Newswires, CNN, Alliance News, Hurriyet Daily News, ING/Saxo Bank commentary via FXStreet, all Sept 8-9): US Central Command said it destroyed **five Iranian oil tankers** near Kharg Island on Tuesday, in response to Iran's IRGC attempting to strike a US Navy warship. Iran retaliated Wednesday with ballistic missiles at a US-used base in Jordan and, per Iranian state media, attacks on **10 ships** near the Strait of Hormuz, while also warning it would target tankers off Kuwait and Bahrain. Separately, Yemen's Iran-backed Houthis — who struck Saudi Aramco's Jazan facilities Tuesday, injuring more than 70 people per Saudi's Foreign Ministry — said Wednesday they had also targeted a 400kb/d Saudi refinery. The UK's maritime security agency (UKMTO) reported a tanker struck by a drone in Iraqi territorial waters and several merchant vessels hit by disabling fire in the Gulf overnight. ING: "we're still some way from a restart in talks... the market is likely to continue to price in a sizeable risk premium." Compounding the supply-side picture: OPEC crude production fell an estimated **900,000 bbl/day** in August to 19.9 million bbl/day, driven by a 1.12 million bbl/day drop in Saudi output as the kingdom rerouted exports away from Hormuz. Goldman Sachs, Bank of America, and UBS all raised their oil-price forecasts this week; Goldman specifically flagged **$120/bbl** as possible if shipping disruptions intensify further. The US Energy Information Administration's September Short-Term Energy Outlook, released today, put its own H2 2026 Brent forecast at **$90/bbl average** — notably below the current spot price, underscoring how much of today's level is a geopolitical risk premium rather than a fundamentals-based forecast. US diesel hit a fresh record **$5.9424/gallon** (AAA data), up from Tuesday's $5.9012. President Trump told reporters energy prices are unlikely to ease before the November midterm election. **This is a live, escalating conflict, not a one-day event** — flagged in every wrap since September 4.

Spot-price wires disagreed more than usual on gold's exact move today, a function of real intraday volatility rather than a data error: a Chinese-language market recap (Flush Financial Services Network) described gold falling as low as **$4,341** before rallying roughly $60-95 to retest **$4,400-4,435** during the session; USA Today's 8:05 AM ET spot read showed gold still down 0.27% at that hour ($4,404.57); by the close, The Sunday Guardian's market-indicator table showed gold **up 1.42%** to $4,417.79. **Why this matters**: the "normal" playbook this month (see the September 4 and September 8 wraps) has been rising yields and rate-hike odds pushing gold down. Today, gold rose (on most readings) despite the 10-year yield touching a post-2023 high and despite an oil-driven inflation scare — a genuine reversal of that pattern. Coverage attributes it to a softer US dollar (DXY ~98.6-98.8, little changed to slightly weaker), Goldman Sachs reaffirming a **$4,900/oz** year-end target citing continued central-bank buying and a fading rate-hike headwind, a Société Générale note calling the 2026 gold market a "full-blown bull market" with simultaneous ETF, futures, and options-position growth (August ETF inflows of 201 tons, the third-largest monthly inflow on record, per the World Gold Council), and a symbolic data point: the Dutch central bank disclosed moving roughly 86 tons of gold reserves from New York and Ottawa to London between March and August, part of a broader "gold repatriation" trend among central banks. Silver and platinum outpaced gold's move (+2.46% and +3.55% respectively) — consistent with a broader precious-metals bid rather than a gold-specific story. **Whether this holds past Thursday's PPI and Friday's CPI is the open question**, not something this report resolves.

## Risks to watch

- **US-Iran / Strait of Hormuz / Saudi energy infrastructure** — actively escalating, not de-escalating. Iran struck a US-used base in Jordan and reported attacks on 10 ships near Hormuz; Houthis claim to have hit a Saudi refinery. Goldman Sachs: oil could reach $120/bbl on further escalation; the EIA's own H2 2026 Brent forecast ($90/bbl average) sits well below the current spot price, meaning a large chunk of today's level is a risk premium that could unwind quickly if talks resume — or extend further if they don't.
- **Thursday's PPI and Friday's CPI** — the fund's next hard data points for the September 15-16 FOMC decision; markets are pricing roughly 58-60% odds of a hike. These prints will also be the real test of whether gold's divergence from the rate-hike playbook (see Commodities) holds.
- **RBC Capital Markets' pullback warning** (published today): strategist Lori Calvasina flagged a possible 5-10% S&P 500 pullback in the weeks ahead, citing September's historically weak seasonality (down in 5 of the past 10 years) and uncertainty ahead of the November 3 midterm elections. A single firm's view, not a consensus call, but worth tracking given the market's third straight down day.
- **US-Canada trade tension** — still unresolved following Canada's retaliatory tariffs (flagged in the September 8 wrap); the White House said it would ban imports of Canadian motorbikes and other products starting later this month.
- **AI/semiconductor complex internal divergence** — Meta and Marvell rallying hard on company-specific AI news while Broadcom and KLA fell with no clear catalyst is worth monitoring for whether it reflects genuine stock-picking (winners vs. laggards within the AI trade) or is noise around Marvell's October 6 investor day.
