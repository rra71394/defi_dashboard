# Global Markets Wrap — September 8, 2026

*Compiled from Bigdata.com (market tearsheet + news search), Twelve Data, and Alpha Vantage. Data as of ~21:30-21:40 UTC, September 8, 2026 (US cash equity close ~20:00 UTC / 4:00 PM ET). This was a normal US trading day — Labor Day (Monday, September 7) is already covered in `reports/2026-09-07-global-markets-wrap.md`.*

## TL;DR

A fresh escalation in the Middle East, not a new US macro print, drove today's tape. Yemen's Houthis attacked Saudi Aramco energy facilities in Jazan this afternoon — on top of the weekend's US strikes on three Iranian oil tankers near the Strait of Hormuz — and crude jumped for a third straight session (Brent briefly approached $100/bbl, WTI to the low-$94s, both +3% on the day). That pushed Treasury yields further up the same path they've been on since Friday's hot jobs report (10-year holding above 4.80%, 2-year at its highest since January 2025, Fed funds futures still pricing ~58-60% odds of a September 15-16 hike), and US equities fell broadly: the Dow led declines at -1.18% (roughly 550-630 points intraday, its worst day in two weeks), the S&P 500 -0.58%, the Nasdaq Composite -0.31%. **Gold fell alongside the risk-off** (-1.70% to $4,400.50/oz per Bigdata.com's tearsheet), because rate-hike repricing is currently outweighing safe-haven demand — the same dynamic flagged in the September 4 wrap.

Two idiosyncratic stories ran against the broader tape. First, the AI/semiconductor complex rallied hard even as the index fell — Intel **+9.05%**, AMD **+5.85%**, Seagate (STX) **+6.37%**, the leveraged Direxion Semiconductor Bull ETF (SOXL) **+5.18%**, Broadcom **+2.98%** — all five are fund watchlist positions, and all five cleared their alert thresholds. Second, a health-care-specific shock hit the Dow and dragged the sector to the bottom of the table: Amgen fell **~9.8-10%** (its worst day since 2000) in sympathy after rival Novartis's competing cholesterol drug (pelacarsen) missed its primary endpoint in a Phase 3 trial, with Novartis itself down ~14%. Neither Amgen nor Novartis is a fund watchlist position, but the sector drag (Health Care -2.50%, the worst S&P sector) is worth knowing.

In crypto, Polkadot (not a watchlist position) spiked as much as +20% intraday on a technical breakout above $1; the fund's own largest crypto story continues to be **Zcash (ZEC)**, still trading in an elevated **~$1,127-1,168** range (Alpha Vantage, 21:39 UTC) as the privacy-coin/short-squeeze/ETF-inflow story flagged in the September 7 wrap keeps running. Tesla (watchlist, **+3.94%**) staged a "moderately bullish," options-flow-driven rebound from Friday's -5.9% drop on the Cybercab NHTSA safety probe — not a new catalyst, per The Fly's options-activity read. Separately, Canada's $20 billion in retaliatory tariffs on US goods took effect today amid stalled trade talks. **This week's PPI (Thursday) and CPI (Friday) remain the next hard data points for the September 15-16 FOMC decision**, more so than anything that happened today.

**The Amgen story, in detail** (source: MT Newswires, Benzinga, Yahoo! Finance, all Sept 8): Novartis reported Friday that its Phase 3 "Lp(a)HORIZON" trial for pelacarsen — a cholesterol-lowering drug targeting lipoprotein(a) — failed to meet its primary endpoint of reducing cardiovascular events. Amgen is developing a similar drug (olpasiran) via a different mechanism, and the market marked it down in sympathy: Amgen fell as much as 9.8-10% intraday, its worst single-day loss since 2000, and BMO Capital downgraded the stock to Market Perform (unchanged $450 target) same-day. UBS pushed back somewhat, arguing olpasiran's different endpoint and higher-risk patient population could still show a ~15% cardiovascular-event reduction, and kept its Buy rating — a genuine, sourced difference of analyst opinion, not a fund view.

**The AI/semiconductor divergence** (source: Yahoo! Finance "Markets News" liveblog, Nikkei Japan, The Fly, all Sept 8, plus Twelve Data quotes below): Intel jumped after Digitimes reported the company plans a further ~10% PC CPU price increase in October; AMD and Broadcom "saw buying" per Nikkei's wrap; Seagate (STX) led a broader memory/storage rally (Roundhill Memory ETF DRAM +2%, iShares Semiconductor ETF SOXX +1.5%) that Yahoo's liveblog did not attribute to a single named catalyst beyond general AI-hardware demand. Qualcomm (+3%, not a watchlist position) rose on a new AWS custom AI-chip supply deal.

**What happened, chronologically** (source: MT Newswires, Reuters/Washington Post, FXStreet, CNBC, Benzinga, all Sept 7-8): Over the weekend, US forces struck and disabled/sank three Iranian oil tankers near Kharg Island and in the Gulf of Oman in retaliation for an Iranian missile attack on two US Navy warships; Iran said it would begin stopping vessels transiting the Strait without its permission, and Kpler tracking data showed Hormuz crossings falling to their lowest level since May (77 vessels last week, down 22-28% week-over-week). On Monday, Iran's IRGC said it captured a US Navy underwater drone at the Strait's entrance (the US called it "defective" and non-sensitive). Today, Houthi forces in Yemen attacked Saudi Aramco energy facilities in the southern Jazan region, causing fires and temporary shutdowns and injuring more than 70 people per Saudi's Foreign Affairs Ministry — the proximate trigger for today's leg up in crude and the synchronized global equity dip described above. The US Treasury separately sanctioned dozens of Iranian airlines today. Goldman Sachs is cited (Benzinga) warning oil could reach $120/bbl if shipping attacks intensify further; Treasury Secretary Bessent has publicly forecast the opposite ($40-50/bbl) once the conflict resolves and alternative pipeline routes reduce Hormuz's importance. Wood Mackenzie separately estimated the conflict is cutting global Q4 crude runs by 1.4 million bpd. **This is a live, unresolved tail risk, not a one-day event** — flagged in every wrap since September 4.
