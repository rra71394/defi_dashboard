## TL;DR

Three separate stories ran today, and none of them is really about the others:

1. **Crypto ripped higher across the board** — Bitcoin broke back above $80,000 (+6.32% to $81,174, Bigdata.com/FMP), and every watchlist crypto pair except one (Zcash, +1.63%) breached its 5% alert threshold, most by a wide margin (Solana +11.61%, Cardano +11.00%, Sui +9.90%, Bitcoin Cash +10.56%). The move traces to two Thursday regulatory developments — the SEC's approval of a five-year conditional exemption for tokenized-stock trading, and the CFTC's formal proposal to regulate crypto asset markets, both read as agencies stepping in after the Senate's Clarity Act failed Tuesday — layered on top of short liquidations (Benzinga cites $170M in 60 minutes early in the session; CoinGlass logged $547.8M liquidated over 24 hours) once Bitcoin cleared the $76,700–78,000 resistance zone. 3. **European equities sold off broadly** (FTSE 100 -1.45%, DAX -1.63%, CAC 40 -1.49%, IBEX 35 -1.67%, all Bigdata.com/FMP) while the US was roughly flat and Asia was firm — a genuine regional divergence. Drivers: a Bank of Japan 25bp hike overnight, an ECB consumer survey showing 12-month euro-area inflation expectations rising to 3.0% (from 2.9%), the French-German 10-year sovereign spread breaking above 100bp for the first time, and a report that Saudi Aramco has told at least two European refinery customers it will suspend crude allocations to them next month over pipeline damage.

**The common thread investors should hold onto:** US Treasury yields spiked back to the 5% level Friday (10-year: +5–7bp intraday to ~5.00–5.006%, per CNBC/MT Newswires, its strongest since 2007) on hawkish commentary from Fed regional presidents and the BOJ hike — normally a headwind for both crypto and equities. That crypto rallied hard on the same day the "risk-free rate" reasserted itself at a 19-year high is a genuine divergence, attributable in the sourcing above to crypto-specific regulatory catalysts and short covering overriding the macro cross-current, not to yields becoming irrelevant. One wire (International Business Times) noted Bitcoin's 24-hour correlation with the S&P 500 has run around 71% recently — today, that correlation broke down.

**Also today:** quarterly "triple witching" (simultaneous expiration of stock options, index options, and index futures), flagged by Alliance News as a contributor to elevated volume and short-term volatility independent of the above.

## Thread 1: Crypto's broad breakout — regulatory catalysts override a 5% Treasury yield

*Source: Benzinga, The Globe and Mail, Morningstar, AOL.com, International Business Times, RTTNews, Finance Magnates, Crypto Economy, Boursorama (Reuters), Crypto Wire, all September 18, 2026.*

Bitcoin traded as high as $81,000+ intraday (Bigdata.com/FMP: $81,174.03, +6.32% 1D) after clearing $76,700 (flagged pre-session as key resistance-turned-support), then $78,000, then $80,000 in succession through the US session. Ethereum ran to ~$2,629.68 (+7.53%), and gains were broad rather than concentrated in majors — the CoinMarketCap Altcoin Season index rose to 44 from Tuesday's low of 32.

**Two distinct regulatory catalysts, both dated Thursday, Sept. 17:**
- The **SEC** introduced a new "Innovation Exemption" / five-year conditional exemptive relief permitting tokenized-stock trading venues to operate.
- The **CFTC** submitted a formal proposal to regulate crypto asset transactions and markets — read alongside the SEC move as coordinated agency action to provide market structure after the Senate rejected the comprehensive Clarity Act bill in a cloture vote on Tuesday, Sept. 15.

That combination triggered a short squeeze once Bitcoin broke technical resistance: Benzinga cites over $170 million in shorts liquidated within a 60-minute window early in the US session, and CoinGlass data (via Benzinga) shows $547.80 million liquidated across 111,660 traders over the trailing 24 hours. SoSoValue reported $159.5 million of net inflows into spot Bitcoin ETFs on Thursday (spot Ethereum ETFs saw a $39.2 million net outflow over the same window).

Crypto-linked equities amplified the move: **Coinbase +11.72%**, **Strategy Inc. +11–13%** ("we're never going to stop buying Bitcoin," per CEO comments cited by Benzinga), **Robinhood +7.8%**, **Canaan +20–22%** (Bitcoin-mining-hardware maker), and mining names Riot Platforms, MARA, Hut 8, and Bit Digital all up 3–10% (Boursorama/Reuters).

**Why this matters against the macro backdrop:** the Fed hiked 25bp Wednesday (first hike since 2023) and the dot plot still shows 16 of 18 officials expecting at least one more 2026 hike; the BOJ hiked 25bp Friday. The 10-year Treasury yield spiked back to ~5.00–5.006% Friday (CNBC, MT Newswires) — normally bearish for non-yielding, speculative assets. International Business Times flagged Bitcoin's 24-hour correlation with the S&P 500 running near 71% recently; today that relationship broke down, with crypto decoupling higher on its own regulatory news while equities stayed roughly flat and yields rose. That is a real divergence, not a data artifact — worth watching for whether it persists or mean-reverts.

## Thread 2: Oil eases for a third day as Saudi supply fears abate — Hormuz still constrained

*Source: CNBC.com, Nasdaq/RTTNews, Yahoo! Finance, MT Newswires - Global Energy, Morningstar, Alliance News, Nikkei Japan, all September 18, 2026.*

Crude fell for a third consecutive session. Saudi Arabia is actively restoring its damaged East-West pipeline (targeting roughly half of lost capacity "within days," full flows in about six weeks per Bloomberg reporting) and has boosted ship-to-ship transfers near Oman's Sohar to 2.7 million barrels per day to route around the outage. Separately, China privately urged Iran to rein in Houthi attacks near the Bab-el-Mandeb strait (Reuters), and President Trump told NewsNation "Iran wants to make a deal," with a Trump–Gulf-leaders meeting planned for Tuesday on the sidelines of the UN General Assembly in New York.

That said, the underlying supply picture is not fully resolved: commercial shipping through the Strait of Hormuz remains "heavily constrained" (MT Newswires), the UK Maritime Trade Operations reported an unknown projectile hitting a tanker in the Strait Friday, and Iran's IRGC said separately it struck a Togo-flagged tanker attempting "illegal passage." Vitol Group estimated the region has lost roughly 2 million bpd of Middle East crude exports plus another 2 million bpd from Russia (Ukrainian drone strikes), and the IEA flagged depleted global inventory buffers (down 2.8 million bpd over six months) as raising the stakes of any further disruption. Saudi Arabia's own August crude exports fell to roughly 3 million bpd — a 9-year low, per Bloomberg/Kpler/Vortexa data cited by Nasdaq/Barchart.

**A sourcing discrepancy worth flagging plainly, not resolving arbitrarily:** the Bigdata.com/FMP tearsheet shows front-month WTI (ticker CLUSD) at **$96.08, -5.72% 1D**, while every news-wire snapshot sourced above — CNBC (WTI settled $100.30, -1.6%), MT Newswires (multiple intraday reads, -1% to -2.3%), Yahoo!/Reuters (-1.8% to -2.3% intraday), and Nikkei Japan ($100.30 close, -1.6%) — puts WTI's close nearer **$100.30, down roughly 1.6–2.3%** on the day. This is a bigger gap than the ordinary intraday-snapshot-timing differences flagged in prior reports (e.g., 9/16, 9/17); it may reflect a different contract reference (front-month vs. a continuous/rolled series) on FMP's side, or a stale FMP print. Brent showed a smaller, more consistent decline: Bigdata.com/FMP has it at $103.87, -0.91%, in line with wire closes clustered around $103.87–$104.87. **Treat the WTI 1D figure from the tearsheet with caution this run; the wire-sourced ~-1.6% to -2.3% range is better corroborated.**

## Thread 3: European equities sell off as BOJ hikes, inflation expectations rise, and Aramco warns of a supply cut

*Source: Nasdaq/RTTNews, Alliance News, Boursorama (Reuters), InfoMoney, Eastmoney (东方财富网), Flush Financial Services Network, MT Newswires, Yahoo!/Investing.com, all September 18, 2026.*

The Stoxx Europe 600 fell roughly 1.0–1.1% and every major European bourse closed lower: FTSE 100 -1.45% to 10,659.13, DAX 40 -1.63% to 25,296.44, CAC 40 -1.49% to 8,065.02, FTSE MIB -1.60%, IBEX 35 -1.67%, per Bigdata.com/FMP and InfoMoney. This stands in sharp contrast to a broadly flat US session (S&P 500 +0.17%) and a firm Asia (Nikkei +1.38%, KOSPI +2.66%, TAIEX +2.90%) — most Asian markets had already closed before the European session's own news flow hit.

Four distinct pressures compounded:
1. **The Bank of Japan raised its policy rate 25 basis points** and warned that underlying inflation could exceed its 2% target on rising wage/price expectations — read as part of a broader global central-bank tightening wave alongside Wednesday's Fed hike.
2. **The ECB's own consumer survey** showed euro-area households' 12-month inflation expectations rising to 3.0% in August from 2.9% in July (against a Bloomberg consensus of 3.1%); medium-term (3-year) expectations also rose, to 2.9% from 2.7%.
3. **Sovereign yield stress**: Boursorama/Reuters reported the French OAT–German Bund 10-year spread broke above 100 basis points for the first time, alongside a broader "global phenomenon" in rising sovereign yields (ECB President Christine Lagarde's characterization, per InfoMoney).
4. **A fresh, Europe-specific oil-supply headline**: per Eastmoney and Flush Financial Services Network (citing unnamed sources), Saudi Aramco has notified at least two European refinery customers that it will suspend all crude oil allocations to them next month, tied to the Red Sea pipeline attack — prompting French President Macron to call for a G7 energy meeting. This lands even as headline crude prices eased (Thread 2), underscoring that the physical-supply story and the price story aren't the same thing.

Sector-level, Stoxx 600 Banks fell 1.4% and Oil & Gas fell 1.1% on the session; Technology was the lone sector gainer (+0.14–0.75% depending on snapshot). In Frankfurt, autos were hit hardest — Volkswagen -5.6%, Porsche/Mercedes-Benz/BMW down 4.4–4.8%.

- **Zcash's sudden underperformance relative to the rest of the crypto book** after two days of >10% gains — could be simple mean-reversion/consolidation, or could signal the earlier short-squeeze/governance-vote catalyst has run its course; worth a scoped follow-up if it persists.
