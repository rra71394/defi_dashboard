# Global Markets Wrap — September 11, 2026

*Compiled from Bigdata.com (market tearsheet + news search) and Twelve Data. Alpha Vantage used once, as a Treasury-yield cross-check. Data as of ~9:35 PM UTC / 5:35 PM ET, September 11, 2026, unless a specific timestamp is given.*

## TL;DR

Markets rallied hard into the close of a volatile week. August CPI came in almost exactly as expected — headline +0.4% m/m (fastest since May), core +0.3% m/m (a four-month high, hotter than the +0.2% consensus), both annual readings in line at 3.4% headline / 2.4% core — and rather than spook the market, the in-line print let it exhale: the S&P 500 gained ~0.9–1.1% intraday, the Nasdaq Composite ~1–1.2%, and the VIX fell over 11% to the mid-15s. This was a **vol-crush, not a dovish repricing** — Fed hike odds for the September 15–16 FOMC actually rose to roughly 69–70% (CME FedWatch) on the CPI print, continuing the hawkish trajectory flagged in yesterday's brief. Simultaneously, Brent and WTI crude fell 2.4–3.3% off multi-month highs after reports that Gulf states and Iran will meet in Oman on September 14 to negotiate a temporary arrangement for shipping through the Strait of Hormuz — a partial de-escalation, not a resolution (Saudi Arabia shut its own East-West pipeline the same day after fresh attacks). Crypto majors rallied alongside equities, led by Ethereum breaking back above $2,600 for the first time in ~8 months.

**Note on context:** this remains a rate-*hike* cycle. A hot-but-in-line inflation print reading as bullish for equities (because it removed uncertainty ahead of next week's Fed decision) rather than as "good news is bad news" is itself a change in market psychology from the September 4 jobs-report reaction — worth watching whether this holds once the Fed actually delivers (or doesn't) next Wednesday.

*Source: Bureau of Labor Statistics data via MT Newswires, Yahoo! Finance/TheStreet, Crypto Briefing — all published September 11, 2026.*

The core beat (0.3% vs 0.2% expected) would normally be read as hawkish friction, but the annual core rate still eased (2.5% → 2.4%), and the headline matched consensus exactly — removing the tail risk of a genuinely hot surprise five days before the Fed meets. The S&P 500's rally on the day was broad: MT Newswires reported all sectors positive except health care and utilities, led by communication services, consumer discretionary and technology. The **CBOE Volatility Index fell to roughly 15.8–15.9** (multiple intraday prints: 15.84 on the Bigdata.com tearsheet at 9:35 PM UTC, 15.88 per Straits Times, 16.33 per an earlier Daily Economic News snapshot) — a real, if modest, vol-crush, though several desks (Nomura, Goldman Sachs' equity vol index, per Wallstreetcn/Yicai wire pieces) flagged that positioning for a volatility *increase* into the September 15–16 FOMC and the November midterms remains unusually elevated, i.e., today's calm may be a pause rather than an all-clear.

Two asset managers cut S&P 500 year-end targets this week despite the rally: **KKR (7,600 → 7,300)** and **UBS (7,700 → 7,500)**, per Barclays-sourced commentary circulating in Ifeng/Gelonghui/Sohu wire coverage — a reminder that the rally is happening against a backdrop of more cautious institutional positioning, not less.

### Rest of world: a session-timing artifact worth flagging

| Country/Index | 1D (ETF, Bigdata.com) | 1D (local cash index) |
|---|---|---|
| Japan | EWJ +2.20% | Nikkei 225 **-1.93%** |
| South Korea | EWY +3.25% | KOSPI **-1.76%** |
| Taiwan | EWT +1.83% | TWII **-1.61%** |

These US-listed country ETFs (EWJ, EWY, EWT) and their local cash indexes diverged sharply — not a real reversal in one direction, but a **session-timing artifact**: the local Asian cash indexes had already closed for their Friday session (still digesting Thursday's four-session US losing streak per a Kabutan Nikkei recap: "the US market saw the Dow fall for the fourth consecutive day... reaching its lowest point since late July"), while the US-listed ETFs continued trading through Friday's US session and picked up the broad risk-on rally described above. Treat the ETF and index figures as two different time windows, not a contradiction. Separately, the **Japanese yen strengthened** this week (~154 → low-153s against the dollar per FXStreet, ~4% appreciation since the start of September) on near-certain pricing of a **25bp BOJ hike to 1.25% at next week's September 17–18 meeting** — a 31-year high for Japanese rates — which is a genuine, if smaller, contributor to the EWJ/JPY-adjusted return gap.

## Commodities

### Energy — a partial de-escalation, not a resolution

| Commodity | Price (Bigdata.com, ~9 PM UTC) | 1D | 5D |
|---|---|---|---|
| WTI Crude | $100.05 | -2.37% | +9.37% |
| Brent Crude | $104.61 | -2.81% | +8.65% |
| Natural Gas | $2.82 | -0.49% | -5.21% |

Crude pulled back from a **four-month intraday high (Brent touched $109.97 per Reuters/Channel NewsAsia, WTI near $103–104)** after the Financial Times reported Gulf states and Iran will hold a foreign-ministers' meeting in Oman on **September 14** to negotiate a temporary arrangement managing shipping through the Strait of Hormuz. Gelber & Associates (quoted via MT Newswires) characterized the pullback as "a partial reduction in the immediate Hormuz risk premium rather than a meaningful improvement in physical supply, with vessel traffic still severely restricted." The de-escalation is fragile: **Saudi Arabia shut its own East-West pipeline** (the bypass route around Hormuz) the same day after attacks in the Riyadh and Medina regions, and Chevron's CEO told an energy conference the price buffers that had capped the rally are "depleted," with risk "to the upside over the next few months." Both benchmarks are still up **>7–9% for the week** — the first weekly close above $100 for WTI since mid-May.

## Risks to watch

- **The September 15–16 FOMC decision** — hike odds sit at ~69–70% (CME FedWatch) after today's CPI; this is now the dominant near-term catalyst for every asset class in this brief.
- **The September 17–18 Bank of Japan meeting** — a 25bp hike to 1.25% (a 31-year high) is fully priced in; the risk is in the forward guidance, where TD Securities flagged a "knee-jerk yen selloff back toward 157–160" if guidance doesn't keep an October/December hike on the table.
- **The September 14 Oman-hosted Gulf–Iran talks** on Strait of Hormuz shipping — today's oil pullback prices in optimism that could reverse quickly; Saudi Arabia's East-West pipeline shutdown the same day is a reminder the underlying conflict has not de-escalated.
- **Elevated volatility positioning despite a low VIX print** — Nomura and Goldman Sachs commentary (via Yicai/Wallstreetcn wire pieces) flagged unusually high demand for VIX three-month call skew (91st percentile) and 17 consecutive trading days in Goldman's "extreme fear" equity-vol regime, the longest streak in 15 years, even as the VIX itself sits near 16.
- **KKR and UBS both cut S&P 500 year-end targets this week** (7,600→7,300 and 7,700→7,500 respectively) — a note of institutional caution underneath today's rally.
