# Global Markets Wrap — September 4, 2026

*Compiled from Bigdata.com (market tearsheet + news search), Twelve Data, and Alpha Vantage. Data as of ~7:05 PM ET / 23:05 UTC, September 4, 2026.*

## TL;DR

One number moved every asset class today. August nonfarm payrolls came in at **+162,000**, nearly triple the ~55,000 Wall Street expected, with unemployment steady at 4.1%. That single print pushed the market's odds of a Fed rate hike at the September 15–16 FOMC meeting from roughly 49% to 58–60% (CME FedWatch), and everything else fell out of it: Treasury yields jumped, the dollar strengthened, gold and crypto sold off, and US stocks slipped even as chip stocks (and Asian markets that had already closed) extended Thursday's tech-led rally. Layered on top of that: crude oil is capping its best week since mid-July on renewed US–Iran hostilities near the Strait of Hormuz, and diesel just hit a record $5.85/gallon.

**Note on context:** this is a rate-*hike* cycle, not a cut cycle — the macro backdrop is a re-acceleration of inflation concerns tied to the oil shock and a war footing in the Gulf, which is why a strong jobs report reads as bad news for risk assets rather than good news.

## The trigger: August payrolls blow past forecasts

| Metric | August | Expected | July (revised) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nonfarm payrolls | **+162,000** | ~+55,000 (survey range 53K–65K) | +21,000 (from an initially reported −23,000) |
| Unemployment rate | 4.1% | 4.1% | 4.1% |
| Avg. hourly earnings, m/m | +0.3% | +0.3% | +0.2% |
| Avg. hourly earnings, y/y | +3.1% (slowest since May 2021) | — | +3.2% |
| Labor force participation | 61.6% | — | 61.4% |
| Fed Sept. 15–16 hike odds (CME FedWatch) | **58–60%** | — | ~49–51% (pre-report) |

June and July were both revised up (net +55,000 combined), and private payrolls (+127,000) and leisure/hospitality (+62,000, rebounding from a decline) both came in hot. The market's read: a labor market this resilient gives the Fed cover to raise rates rather than cut, especially with energy-driven inflation still running above target. Next week's CPI and PPI, not today's jobs number, are now the real swing factor for the September decision.

**The cascade:** NFP beat → Fed hike odds jump → Treasury yields and the dollar rise → gold and crypto (both non-yielding, dollar-sensitive) sell off → US equities dip on "good news is bad news," cushioned only by chip stocks that rallied on their own AI-demand story.

### Energy — the week's story, not the day's

| Commodity | Price | 1D | 5D (≈week) |
|---|---|---|---|
| WTI Crude | $91.48 | +0.20% | **+9.69%** |
| Brent Crude | $96.28 | +0.80% | **+9.28%** |
| Natural Gas | $2.98 | +2.13% | +3.01% |
| Gasoline (RBOB) | $3.20 | +2.07% | +4.90% |
| Heating Oil | $4.55 | −1.01% | +7.01% |

Crude is capping its **strongest weekly gain since mid-July**, driven by renewed US–Iran hostilities threatening the Strait of Hormuz — a chokepoint for roughly one-fifth of global oil flows. President Trump said the US may strike Iran's "Pickaxe Mountain" site near the Natanz enrichment facility. Today itself was comparatively calm (crude roughly flat to slightly down) as Saudi Aramco held its October official selling prices unchanged for Asian buyers — a signal of adequate supply — and traders positioned ahead of **Sunday's OPEC+ meeting**, where the group is expected to leave October output policy unchanged. The more acute story is **diesel**, which hit a **record $5.85/gallon** nationally (+60% year-over-year), squeezed by both the Hormuz risk and Russia–Ukraine war disruptions to refined-product flows.

Crypto's selloff is the most direct read-through of the jobs report of any asset class: a hotter labor market lowers the odds of easier Fed policy, which is bearish for a non-yielding, speculative asset class. This reverses a sharp Wednesday–Thursday rally in which Bitcoin broke above **$82,000 for the first time in more than three months**, fueled by over **$3 billion in August institutional/ETF inflows**. Crypto-proxy equities amplified the move unevenly: **Bitmine** (an Ethereum treasury vehicle) fell **6%**, far more than a mechanical read of ETH's decline would suggest, while **Strategy/MSTR** (a Bitcoin treasury vehicle) fell a milder 1–3% — a gap that points to more crowded, momentum-driven positioning unwinding in the smaller, newer name.

## Risks to watch

- **Next week's CPI and PPI** — the real decider for the September 16 FOMC decision, more so than today's jobs report.
- **Sunday's OPEC+ meeting** on October output policy.
- **Strait of Hormuz escalation risk** — any further US–Iran exchange could push oil sharply higher again.
- **The Bank of Japan's meeting in roughly two weeks** — markets are pricing a 75–87% chance of a rate hike, which has already been driving yen strength; strategists flagged a yen carry-trade unwind as a contained-for-now but real tail risk.
- One wire commentary flagged a more extreme scenario — a financial-crisis-scale unwind tied to AI-valuation risk arriving between late 2026 and H1 2027 — citing the August 19 "warning shot" session (KOSPI fell nearly 7% and briefly tripped a circuit breaker, Nikkei fell over 3%). This is a minority view in the coverage, not a consensus call, but it's a reminder of how thin the risk premium is after this year's run-up.
