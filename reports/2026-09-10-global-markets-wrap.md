# Global Markets Wrap — September 10, 2026

*Compiled from Bigdata.com (market tearsheet + news search), Twelve Data, and Alpha Vantage. Data as of ~21:35-21:40 UTC, September 10, 2026 (US cash equity close ~20:00 UTC / 4:00 PM ET).*

## TL;DR

A hot US inflation print collided with a fourth straight day of an escalating Iran/Strait-of-Hormuz oil shock. August's Producer Price Index rose **5.4% year-over-year** (above the 5.3% forecast, the highest reading since May), and that print alone pushed the market's odds of a September 15-16 Fed rate hike from **61% to 70%** intraday (CME FedWatch, via multiple wires). Layered on top: Brent crude briefly topped **$108/bbl** intraday (+7.57% on the day) and WTI hit **$102.48** (+6.69%), both continuing the escalation flagged in every wrap since September 4 — the US and Iran continue to trade strikes near the Strait of Hormuz, and OPEC cut its 2026 demand outlook for a fifth straight month, citing high prices. The combination pushed the **10-year Treasury yield to ~4.95%** (a post-2023 high) and the **30-year to ~5.37%** (a level last seen in 2007), and US equities fell for a **fourth consecutive session** — the S&P 500's longest losing streak since June: S&P 500 **-0.58%** to 7,591.70, Dow **-0.60%**, Nasdaq Composite **-0.65%**. The VIX jumped **+8.38%** to 17.84, touching an intraday high near 18.11 (+10%) — still a moderate absolute level, but Nomura strategists flagged a "negative risk trinity" (seasonal VIX weakness, midterm-election risk, and the Iran war) driving unusually expensive volatility hedges.

**Semiconductors were the session's sharpest single-sector casualty**: the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index (SOX) fell **more than 3%**, with Intel and Lam Research down **more than 5%**, Arm and KLA down **more than 4%**, and AMD, ASML, and Qualcomm all down **around 3%** — confirmed on the fund's own book, **AMD fell -3.36%** (Twelve Data), breaching its 3% alert threshold. **Precious metals fell in tandem with gold**, an unusual break from metals' recent divergent pattern (see September 9 wrap): silver **-5.42%**, platinum **-6.14%**, palladium **-6.25%**, all pressured by the stronger dollar and higher real yields, with platinum/palladium carrying an added structural headwind — the World Platinum Investment Council this week revised its 2026 forecast from a supply deficit to an **8.2-ton surplus** on heavy ETF outflows. Crypto sold off alongside risk assets: Bitcoin fell below **$77,000** intraday, and one wire cited **over 160,000 traders liquidated** market-wide in 24 hours.

Both benchmarks are up roughly **16% in September alone**, having already gained in July and August (Flush Financial Services Network). Yahoo Finance's midday recap put Brent at "over $106" with a further intraday push to **above $108** by the close. The escalation is unchanged in kind from prior wraps but has intensified: OPEC cut its global 2026 oil demand growth forecast for a **fifth straight month** (to 380,000 bbl/day, from 580,000 previously) citing high prices weighing on consumption, even as OPEC's own August production rose. President Trump, addressing the political fallout, said elevated prices could persist "through the upcoming midterm elections" before easing, and separately the EIA raised its own 2026 price forecasts (Brent to $91/bbl average, WTI to $84.65) — notably still well below the current spot price, a gap that continues to reflect a geopolitical risk premium rather than a fundamentals-based view. One prediction-market data point (Crypto Briefing, citing Gloria Finance): the probability WTI hits $100 in September moved to **100%**, up from 44% just 24 hours earlier.

## Risks to watch

- **US-Iran / Strait of Hormuz — actively escalating, unresolved.** This is the fourth consecutive wrap flagging live conflict, not de-escalation. Trump said prices could stay elevated "through the midterms" before easing; the EIA's own forecast ($91/bbl Brent average for 2026) sits well below the current ~$108 spot, meaning a large share of today's level is a risk premium that could unwind quickly on any de-escalation, or extend further if the conflict deepens.
- **Friday's CPI print** — the next hard data point for the September 15-16 FOMC decision, and the number markets will use to confirm or walk back today's jump to 70% hike odds.
- **The semiconductor/memory-sector selloff (SOX -3%+)** — worth tracking whether this is a one-day repricing on rates/inflation or the start of a deeper AI/semis drawdown; the fund carries meaningful direct exposure here (NVDA, AMD, INTC, MU, AVGO, TSM, KLAC, MRVL, SOXL among watchlist names).
- **The EWY vs. KOSPI snapshot mismatch** flagged in the Equities section — worth a cleaner same-basis comparison in tomorrow's run if the divergence persists, since it materially changes how "South Korea" reads as a single data point.
- **Platinum/palladium's structural oversupply story (WPIC's revised 8.2-ton surplus forecast)** — layered on top of today's macro-driven selloff, this could mean platinum/palladium underperform gold/silver on any metals-sector recovery, not just on the way down.
- **VIX's "negative risk trinity"** (seasonal weakness, midterm elections, Iran war) flagged explicitly by Nomura and cited by CNBC/MSN — a reminder that options markets are already pricing more volatility ahead, not just reacting to today's move.
