## TL;DR

A week that started with an Iran-driven oil relief rally (Friday, Sept 25) reversed hard today. President Trump rejected Iran's proposal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz within seven days, and crude spiked as much as roughly $4/barrel intraday before paring back — WTI settled at $92.60 (+0.21%) and Brent at $105.28 (+0.92%), though intraday prints ran far hotter (Moneycontrol/Reuters cited Brent up as much as 3.29% to $107.75 before the pullback). That kept inflation fears alive just as a blowout US S&P Global Composite PMI (58.4 flash, vs. 56.0 prior and 55.3 expected — a 62-month high) and a weak University of Michigan sentiment read (48.1 final, with 1-year inflation expectations jumping to 4.6% from 4.0%) reinforced a hawkish Fed backdrop. Treasury yields surged to multi-decade highs: the 10-year hit 5.241%, its highest since June 2007, and the 30-year hit 5.561%, its highest since June 2002 — both fresh 52-week highs, with the 2-year up more than 50bp in September alone (largest monthly rise since February 2023). Gold and silver got hit by the same higher-yields/stronger-dollar dynamic despite the risk-off tone — gold fell 3.54% to $4,168.40/oz and silver fell 4.76% to $61.72/oz, both citing rate expectations and a firmer dollar (DXY ~101.2) rather than a flight to safety. The one clean outlier: **Chainlink (LINK) rallied 8.56%** to a 2026 high on its own idiosyncratic story (CCIP 2.0 launch, an Infosys partnership, and sustained whale accumulation), completely decoupled from the rest of crypto, which sold off with everything else. Nvidia bucked the tech-sector slide too, rising 1.68–2.8% (sources vary) after its board authorized a record $150 billion buyback increase, the largest in history, lifting total remaining authorization to $235 billion.

**Note on context:** this is the same rate-hike cycle flagged in reports since September 16 (the Fed's first hike since 2023, with most policymakers projecting at least one more this year). Today is a re-acceleration of that pressure, not a new regime — the Friday, Sept 25 relief rally on Iran's diplomatic overture proved short-lived once Washington rejected the terms.

## The trigger: Iran rejection reopens the inflation trade; a hot PMI and soft consumer sentiment add a stagflationary edge

*Source: MSN, Channel NewsAsia/Devdiscourse, Edaily, Yonhap News, Nasdaq (Zacks), Morningstar Data Talk, WHEC TV-10, Business Standard, MSN/Livemint/Moneycontrol/The Economic Times/The Sunday Guardian (India wire coverage), Eastmoney/Securities Times/21st Century Business (China wire coverage), all September 28, 2026.*

President Trump rejected Iran's Friday proposal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz within seven days in exchange for US concessions, reversing the relief that had rallied markets into the weekend. Oil jumped on the news — Yonhap and Moneycontrol both described a sharp intraday surge (as much as ~$4/barrel, with Brent briefly quoted up 3.29% to $107.75) — before paring back to a comparatively modest close (WTI +0.21% to $92.60, Brent +0.92% to $105.28 per the Bigdata.com/FMP tearsheet, which multiple wire settlement prints corroborated). Trump did tell Axios both sides would return to negotiations this week, but the immediate market read was that the standoff, and its inflation risk, is unresolved.

That kept the inflation narrative alive just as the US S&P Global Composite PMI flash for September came in at 58.4 (vs. 56.0 in August and 55.3 expected) — a 62-month high, with both services and manufacturing showing synchronized strength (Eastmoney/Securities Times, citing the PMI release). Separately, the University of Michigan's final September consumer sentiment index came in at 48.1 (vs. a preliminary 47.8 and August's 51.7), with the 1-year inflation expectations sub-index rising to 4.6% from 4.0% and the 5-year measure edging up to 3.4% from 3.3% (Nasdaq/Zacks) — a combination of weak sentiment and rising inflation expectations that reads as stagflationary rather than simply hawkish.

Treasury yields responded with a fresh leg higher across the curve: the 10-year yield rose to 5.241% (+0.061 point on the day), its highest since June 12, 2007, marking a fifth consecutive day of gains and its largest 5-day yield gain since April 2025 (Morningstar Data Talk). The 30-year hit 5.561% (+0.061 point), its highest since June 10, 2002. The 2-year, most sensitive to Fed expectations, rose to 4.937% (Edaily) and is up more than 50 basis points in September alone — the largest monthly rise since February 2023 (Channel NewsAsia/Devdiscourse). CME FedWatch-implied odds of an October 28 hike were reported at 68% (Reuters/Moneycontrol), 70.3% (Yonhap), and ~70% elsewhere — consistent enough this run to state as "roughly 70%" without the wide dispersion flagged in the September 25 report.

**The cascade:** Trump rejects Iran's Hormuz proposal → oil spikes intraday before settling only modestly higher → a blowout PMI and weak, inflation-anxious consumer sentiment reinforce the hawkish case → Treasury yields hit multi-decade highs across the curve → equities fall broadly and the VIX jumps → gold and silver, normally a risk-off hedge, instead sell off hard on the higher-real-yields/stronger-dollar mechanism → the semiconductor and AI-adjacent complex (ARM, Qualcomm, Intel, SK Hynix, Meta, AMD, Marvell, SanDisk per Eastmoney's wire roundup) leads the equity decline, partly offset by Nvidia's idiosyncratic buyback news.
