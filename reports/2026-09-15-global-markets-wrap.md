## TL;DR

Three separate stories collided into a second straight down day for US and global equities, on the eve of Wednesday's FOMC decision:

1. **Saudi Arabia's oil-supply crisis got worse, not better.** Riyadh canceled September crude cargoes to European customers and suspended loadings at its Yanbu port after last week's drone attack shut the East-West pipeline (the main bypass around the Strait of Hormuz). Physical oil in Europe spiked above $130/bbl (North Sea Forties hit $136.75, within reach of April's $147.37 record) even as futures benchmarks rose more moderately: WTI +4.38% to $105.83, Brent +2.90% to $108.75. US diesel hit a fresh record $6.2694/gallon.
2. **The Fed decision is tomorrow, and the bond market front-ran it.** The 10-year Treasury yield briefly touched **5.04% intraday — its highest level since 2007** — before easing back to ~5.00%, as CME FedWatch odds of a 25-basis-point hike climbed to 92–95%. This would be the Fed's first rate *hike* since July 2023 (it has only cut or held since) and its first change of any kind since December 2025.
3. **Crypto sold off sharply after the CLARITY Act failed in the Senate.** The crypto market-structure bill fell short on a procedural vote, 49–50 — missing not just the 60 votes needed to break a filibuster but a simple majority, a structurally worse outcome than markets had priced. Bitcoin fell as much as 5% intraday to briefly dip below $75,000; XRP was hit hardest among majors, down nearly 10%.

A partial counter-current: **chip stocks rebounded** from Monday's AI-slowdown-driven selloff after Broadcom CEO Hock Tan pushed back on fears of an AI-capex pullback — a genuine divergence from the rest of the risk-off tape that matters for the fund's book (see Portfolio read).

**Note on context:** precious metals fell *despite* the oil-driven inflation scare and the Middle East backdrop that would normally support a haven bid — for the second session in a row (see Sept. 14's wrap), the Fed-hike/stronger-dollar dynamic dominated gold and silver pricing rather than geopolitics or inflation fear.

## Thread 1: Oil supply crisis deepens — Saudi cancels cargoes, Yanbu suspended

*Source: MT Newswires (Global Energy), FXStreet, Nasdaq, Benzinga, The Economic Times, Time Magazine, Hurriyet Daily News, Tv Asahi, all September 15, 2026.*

Saudi Arabia canceled some September crude cargoes to European customers and suspended loadings at Yanbu — the port at the end of its 7-million-bbl/day East-West pipeline, the kingdom's main route for bypassing the Strait of Hormuz — after Thursday's drone attack (blamed on Iraqi militias, which Tehran denies) shut the pipeline down. Regional officials estimate repair at three to five weeks; US Energy Secretary Chris Wright said Tuesday the pipeline would resume "very soon" and that some Hormuz exports had restarted with US military support.

The loss of the Red Sea/Yanbu route pushed European buyers, including Poland, to scramble for alternative crude, and physical prices spiked well above exchange-traded futures: North Sea Forties crude hit **$136.75/bbl**, approaching the April 13 record of $147.37, and European physical cargoes traded above **$130/bbl** — a much sharper move than the futures benchmarks below.

Compounding the Saudi squeeze: Houthi forces struck the Khamis Mushait military airbase in southern Saudi Arabia in retaliation for Saudi strikes in Yemen; Libya's National Oil Corporation suspended operations at three fields after a protest shut a valve on the Hamada-Zawiya export pipeline; and Ukraine struck Russia's Syzran refinery overnight, adding to a separate diesel-supply squeeze from the Russia-Ukraine war. US retail fuel prices hit fresh records: diesel **$6.2694/gal** (AAA, vs. $3.6858 a year ago) and regular gasoline **$4.3289/gal** (vs. $3.1777 a year ago).

Analyst forecasts widened materially on the news: Goldman Sachs sees oil as high as $120/bbl if attacks on shipping intensify (vs. $80 if exports normalize); JPMorgan estimates each additional month of disruption adds $7–8/bbl to Brent; Rabobank raised its Brent Q4 2026 forecast to $107 and flagged a retest of the year's $126 high if the outage extends.

## Thread 2: 10-year Treasury yield hits highest since 2007 ahead of Wednesday's Fed decision

*Source: FXStreet, CBS News, Alliance News, MT Newswires, Yonhap News, Boursorama/Reuters, all September 15, 2026.*

The FOMC's two-day meeting began Tuesday, with a decision due Wednesday at 2:00 PM ET followed by Chair Kevin Warsh's press conference at 2:30 PM ET. The CME FedWatch tool priced the odds of a 25-basis-point hike at **92–95%** by Tuesday's close (up from ~69% Friday), which would mark the Fed's first hike since July 2023 and its first rate change of any kind since December 2025. Markets are now treating the hike itself as fully priced — the real swing factor is the updated Summary of Economic Projections (the "dot plot") and whether Warsh signals more tightening is coming. Goldman Sachs expects the dot plot to show a narrow majority (10 members) leaning toward just one hike this year; UBS and others see risk skewed toward two.

## Thread 3: Crypto sells off as the CLARITY Act fails in the Senate

*Source: Yahoo! Finance, Forbes.com, MT Newswires (Crypto), Crypto Wire, Crypto Briefing, Sohu, AOL.com, iProUP, all September 15, 2026.*

The Digital Asset Market Clarity Act — the crypto industry's top legislative priority, which would create a federal regulatory framework for digital assets — failed a Senate procedural vote **49–50** Tuesday afternoon. That's a materially worse outcome than the market had priced: falling short of the 60 votes needed to break a filibuster is one thing, but failing to reach even a simple majority (Republicans' own bloc wasn't unified) suggests, per one regulatory-risk analyst quoted in iProUP, that rebuilding support "takes an entire legislative cycle" rather than a quick renegotiation. Polymarket odds on the bill passing in 2026 had already fallen from ~34% Monday to 17% by Tuesday morning, so much of the bad news was pre-priced — the reaction was described by Yahoo! Finance as "orderly rather than a rush for the exits." With roughly three weeks of legislative days left before midterm campaigning dominates Congress's calendar, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent's fallback — SEC/CFTC rulemaking — is now the nearest thing to a 2026 regulatory timeline left for US crypto markets.

Crypto was already softening earlier in the session on Fed-hike jitters and the 10-year's push through 5%, then extended losses sharply after the vote:
